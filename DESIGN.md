# 1. THE IDEA — "The Lake Effect"

Most contractor websites are a surname, a truck photo, and a phone number. This one gets a
concept, because this business already has something none of its competitors do: **a
character, a place, and a calendar.**

> **Western New York doesn't have four seasons. It has four moods — and Dave Schultz has a
> service for every one of them.**
>
> Build the site as a **living almanac of the Southtowns year.** It should feel different in
> February than it does in July, because for the people in Hamburg and Blasdell, February *is*
> different. Lake-effect snow is not weather here; it is identity.

This is the ownable idea. It's unfakeable — a franchise in another state literally cannot copy
"we know what your driveway looks like in January." It is also *useful*: it surfaces the right
service at the right moment, which is exactly how this business actually earns money.

### The two worlds

The company has two temperaments, and the site should physically feel the difference as you
move between them.

| | **THE BEAR** — outside | **THE BIRD** — inside |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Dave Schultz. 50 years in Hamburg. Known locally as "The Bear." | Nichole Pigeon, RIT Design School graduate. Leads the Pigeon Division. |
| Domain | Plowing · pressure washing · landscaping · gutters · house clearance · sunrooms | Design · trim · cabinets · tile · paint · finish carpentry |
| Feeling | Cold, bright, high contrast. Weather. Muscle. Gets there at 4am. | Warm, quiet, generous white space. Craft. Considered. |
| Palette | Navy · charcoal · snow-white · safety yellow | Warm grey · tan · terracotta · blueprint blue |
| Type | Condensed, uppercase, industrial | Lighter weights, more letter-spacing, more air |
| Motion | Decisive. Snap. Weight. | Slow. Settled. Nothing hurries. |

**Hierarchy is explicit: the Bear is the brand. The Bird is a division of it.**

⚠️ **Match how Dave already does this — he has it right, don't over-build past him.** On his
current site the Pigeon Division is:
- a **top-level nav item sitting alongside the other services**, not a sub-site or a separate brand
- on the **same page template** as everything else
- framed as a **welcome/announcement** — *"Now… introducing the Pigeon Division!"*
- explicitly *"an expanded interior and exterior construction **division**"* of D.S. Handymen Inc.
- proved with a **large before/after gallery** — his page carries ~99 images, and his own caption
  is *"These before and after pictures speak for themselves!"*
- closed with the **same phone number and the same free-estimate CTA** as every other page —
  no separate contact path, no separate identity

So: the Bird gets her own mascot, her own chat assistant, and a **warmer tint** on her page —
but she does **not** get a separate world, a portal, or a full visual re-skin. Think *"the
same house, a warmer room"* — not a different building. Dave's own line is the anchor:
**"The Bear and the Bird work together."**

### The delight moment
The two chat assistants **hand off to each other.** Ask the Bear about backsplash tile and he
says *"That's the Bird's department — hang on."* The panel warms, the avatar changes, and
Nichole's assistant takes over. That single interaction communicates the entire business
structure better than any org chart, and nobody in this market has anything like it.

scroll markers** · **a feather trail as the Bird's equivalent** · blueprint grid on Pigeon pages.

# 6. SIGNATURE INTERACTIONS — the things that make it memorable

Build these. They are the difference between "nice contractor site" and "I'm showing my wife this."

**① The Season Engine.** The homepage hero, accent imagery and featured service shift across
four states — **Deep Winter · Thaw · Green · Leaf-Fall.** Drive it from the visitor's date
(optionally refine with a free weather API for WNY, but **date alone must be sufficient**).
Every state must be **server-rendered HTML by default** — the JS only swaps. Fully crawlable,
works with JS off.

**② The Snow Gauge.** A vertical measuring stick that fills with snow as you scroll the plowing
page, marked in inches. His own Facebook infographic already does exactly this — you're
translating something he made into the site. Ends at a CTA: *"Get on the list."*

**③ The Stamp.** The circular badge behaves like a physical rubber stamp — on section reveal it
presses in with a short, weighty motion and a faint ink-edge. Once or twice per page. **Never**
on the Pigeon pages, where nothing should be that forceful.

**④ The Real Before/After.** `beforeafter-siding-windows-BEFORE/AFTER.jpg` is the **same house**
— yellow with old windows and a Bills flag flying, then blue with new siding and windows and the
hedge cleared. Drag-to-compare, big, above the fold on the homepage. This is real work by his
real crew and it is the most persuasive asset in the entire library.

**⑤ Tune In.** He has a professionally produced **Buffalo radio commercial** sitting unused.
Give it a small vintage-radio module: *"Hear our radio ad."* Never autoplay.

**⑥ The Handoff.** The two assistants passing you between them (see §8).

**⑦ Warm Room.** The Pigeon Division page runs a warmer tint of the same design system — warm
grey and terracotta accents, slower motion, more air, lighter type weights. **Same house,
warmer room.** No portal, no full re-skin, no separate identity: header, footer, nav and phone
number stay exactly as they are everywhere else.

> **Discipline:** all of the above must respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and must not cost
> layout stability or LCP. A beautiful site that jitters on a phone in a Hamburg driveway is a
> failed site.
